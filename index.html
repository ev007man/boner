5 things Tuberville said on data centers, Medicaid and taxes in Alabama

  • Published: Oct. 16, 2026, 12:17 p.m.
By
  • Ruth Serven Smith | rserven@al.com

U.S. Sen. Tommy Tuberville said data center development can help Alabama, and said in recent comments in Birmingham that if elected governor, he’ll work to cut red tape for business.

In a speech to a Birmingham Rotary Club, he said if he wins the Nov. 3 election, he’ll “structure everything for business” in the state and try to make it easier to get permits, build and start projects.

“We’re way overtaxed in this state,” he said.

Tuberville spoke about plans to help business and address pressing issues in Alabama. He also took questions about how he addresses critics and why he won’t debate Democratic opponent Doug Jones.

Jones has said he wants a moratorium on new data center development, to phase out a school choice program and to expand Medicaid.

Here are five subjects he discussed:

Data centers

Earlier this summer, Tuberville stated support for data center development and called criticism “bull crap.” Recently, he moderated his position in a new ad. In Birmingham, he said he still believes a recent surge in development will help Alabama.

“We’re looking at several other things we can do to bring money in; everybody brings up these data centers.”

“It’s one of the few ways that we can make millions of dollars for this state,” he said.

He cited an estimate that an unnamed data center will bring in millions for local schools. In Birmingham, the Nebius AI factory says it will generate $87 million in revenue for local schools annually. Other projects in Tuscaloosa County and Lowndes County also promise millions for schools.

“A data center is nothing but a manufacturing plant,” Tuberville said.

“Nothing but manufacturing but for some reason people have been reading all the social media saying it’s bad, it uses too much water, it’s too noisy, it uses too much energy.”

He repeated promises that utility companies that are powering data centers will not pass on costs to local consumers.

Taxes and workforce

Tuberville said he wants to be able to grow the state’s revenue without raising taxes. He has previously said he’s interested in cutting property taxes and personal and corporate income taxes in the state.

In Birmingham, he said he’ll work to cut the grocery tax and said he wants to “drop as much as we possibly can the state income tax.”

He also said that he wants to help recruit more high-quality white collar and blue collar employers to the state.

Education and lottery

In other comments, Tuberville said he wants to make it easier for Alabama students to stay in the state and get good jobs.

He repeated praise for the CHOOSE Act and Alabama’s school choice programs.

And he said he’ll support a lottery if the Legislature votes a package through.

Medicaid expansion

In response to a question, Tuberville said he doesn’t think the money is there to expand Medicaid.

“Unfortunately, if we were to try to expand, there is no possible way we could find a way to pay for it,” he said.

He cited work in Congress with U.S. Sen. Katie Britt and President Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill, which has sent some funding to rural hospitals in Alabama.

He said that funding could provide a stopgap while the state works on a solution other than Medicaid expansion.

Tuberville suggested that if elected, he might try to expand the number of mental health beds, improve prison healthcare and take some of the strain off of small, rural hospitals.

“Healthcare is a huge need,” he said.

Debating Doug Jones

In response to a question about why he hasn’t debated Jones, Tuberville said the two candidates have “nothing in common.”

He said he has laid out his ideas for governor to groups like the Rotary Club across the state.

“There’s no reason to debate because that’s what I’m going to do,” he said. “I just told you what I what I what I stand for, the reasons I stand for it and what I want to happen. You got to have a plan, you got to have a a playbook and you got to have team members. And so that’s what I’ve been trying to do.”
